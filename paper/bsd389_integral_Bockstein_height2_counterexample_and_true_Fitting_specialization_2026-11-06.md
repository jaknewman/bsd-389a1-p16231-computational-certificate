## 1. New source-verified scope of published anticyclotomic Fitting theorems

Chan-Ho Kim, *On the Fitting ideals of anticyclotomic Selmer groups of elliptic curves with good ordinary reduction*, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 69 (2026), Theorem 1.1, proves for **the stated hypotheses**
\[
\mathrm{Fitt}_{0,\Lambda_n}
 \bigl(\mathrm{Sel}(K_n,E[p^\infty])^\vee\bigr)
 =
 (\theta_n,\nu_{n-1,n}\theta_{n-1})^2 .
\]
The hypotheses include (Im), (Ram), (Spl), (Na), (Def) **and the explicit field restriction \(D_K>4\)**, because the article assumes \(K\) has odd discriminant \(-D_K<-4\) (PDF printed pp.21–22). The project's fixed \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\) **does not satisfy** this field hypothesis. Nor does the theorem by itself identify the second Fitting ideal at the augmentation for rank two.

Da Ronche–Longo–Vigni, *Higher Fitting ideals and the structure of anticyclotomic Shafarevich–Tate groups* (arXiv:2603.12357, March 2026), prove in the definite case an equivalence
\[
\mathrm{Fitt}_{i,\Lambda}(\mathcal X)\sim\mathfrak C_i^2
\quad(i\ \text{even}),
\]
where \(\mathcal X\) is a torsion \(\Lambda\)-module from the anticyclotomic Selmer group, \(\mathfrak C_i\) is generated by **variable auxiliary prime** bipartite Euler-system elements, and \(\sim\) is **equivalence up to height-two ideals** (equivalently, pseudo-isomorphism information). Their hypotheses differ and include the ordinary and nonanomalous regime. This theorem is relevant to the missing higher Fitting information, but **an equality up to height-two factors cannot by itself control a p-primary finite defect supported at the maximal ideal \((p,T)\)**; in addition, transfer to the classical Q-Selmer requires a precise control theorem and the quadratic +/- decomposition.

## 2. Universal exact pair of 4×4 matrices

Let p be **any prime**, \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z_p[[T]]\) and
\[
B_{\mathrm{bad}}(T)=
\begin{pmatrix}p&T\\T&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
A_{\mathrm{bad}}(T)=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&T&0&0\\ T&0&0&0\\
0&0&p&T\\ 0&0&T&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
A_{\mathrm{good}}(T)=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,T^2,T^2).
\]
Set \(X_j=\mathrm{coker}_{\Lambda} A_j\), for \(j=\mathrm{good},\mathrm{bad}\).

**Theorem 1.** Both \(X_j\) are finitely presented torsion \(\Lambda\)-modules, **torsion-free as \(\mathbf Z_p\)-modules**, with the exact same principal initial Fitting ideal:
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Fitt}_{0,\Lambda}(X_{\mathrm{good}})
=\mathrm{Fitt}_{0,\Lambda}(X_{\mathrm{bad}})
=(T^4)
=(\theta^2),\qquad \theta=T^2.
}
\]
Their leading theta coefficient is \(1\), a unit. For **every finite layer**
\(\Lambda_n=\mathbf Z_p[T]/((1+T)^{p^n}-1)\) the base-change formula gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Fitt}_{0,\Lambda_n}(X_{\mathrm{good}}\otimes\Lambda_n)
=\mathrm{Fitt}_{0,\Lambda_n}(X_{\mathrm{bad}}\otimes\Lambda_n)
=(T^4)\Lambda_n.
}
\]
Thus knowing ALL initial Fitting ideals at ALL finite layers, and their square-root theta with a quadratic unit, does not distinguish the two families.

**Proof:** \(\det A_{\mathrm{good}}=T^4\) and
\(\det A_{\mathrm{bad}}=(-T^2)^2=T^4\). For a square presentation Fitt0 is generated by its determinant; Fitting ideals commute with base change. \(X_{\mathrm{good}}\simeq(\Lambda/T^2)^2\), Zp-free of rank four. For one B_bad block, write generators u,v with relations \(Tu=0\) and \(Tv=-pu\); there are no nonzero relations among u,v over Zp. Therefore the block cokernel is also Zp-free rank two (with nilpotent T action), and \(X_{\mathrm{bad}}\) is Zp-free rank four. QED.

**Theorem 2.** Specialization at the augmentation \(T=0\) yields **opposite torsion conclusions at the same rank two**:
\[
\boxed{
X_{\mathrm{good}}/TX_{\mathrm{good}}\simeq\mathbf Z_p^2,\qquad
X_{\mathrm{bad}}/TX_{\mathrm{bad}}\simeq
  \mathbf Z_p^2\oplus(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^2.
}
\]
The two finite p-primary defects are, respectively, **zero** and a perfect-square order \(p^2\). The latter admits a standard hyperbolic alternating pairing, so it respects the Cassels–Tate square-order constraint.

**Proof:** Specialization of a finitely presented cokernel is the cokernel of the specialized matrix. At T=0,
\(A_{\mathrm{good}}(0)=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0,0)\), whereas
\(A_{\mathrm{bad}}(0)=\mathrm{diag}(p,0,p,0)\).
The cokernels follow. QED.

**Theorem 3.** The exact higher Fitting ideal catches the defect:
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Fitt}_{2,\Lambda}(X_{\mathrm{good}})=\Lambda,\qquad
\mathrm{Fitt}_{2,\Lambda}(X_{\mathrm{bad}})=(p,T)^2.
}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Fitt}_{2,\mathbf Z_p}(X_{\mathrm{good}}/TX_{\mathrm{good}})=\mathbf Z_p,\qquad
\mathrm{Fitt}_{2,\mathbf Z_p}(X_{\mathrm{bad}}/TX_{\mathrm{bad}})=p^2\mathbf Z_p.
}
\]
**Proof:** Fitt2 of a 4×4 matrix is generated by its 2×2 minors. The good matrix has a minor 1. For the bad matrix, its nonzero 2×2 minors are among \(p^2,pT,T^2,-T^2\), and each of \(p^2,pT,T^2\) actually occurs, so their ideal is \((p,T)^2\). Base-change \(T\mapsto0\) proves the second display. QED.

**Theorem 4 (codimension-two obstruction is not even eliminated by pseudo-isomorphism).** At the unique height-one prime (T) on the support of both modules, p is a unit, and each bad 2×2 block is equivalent under invertible row/column operations to \(\mathrm{diag}(1,T^2)\). Thus
\[
X_{\mathrm{good},(T)}\cong X_{\mathrm{bad},(T)};
\]
both vanish at other height-one primes. The two modules are pseudo-isomorphic over \(\Lambda\), and \(X_{\mathrm{bad}}\) has **no finite \(\Lambda\)-submodule** because it is Zp-torsion-free. Nevertheless the augmentation fibers differ by \((\mathbf Z/p)^2\). The difference is localized in the **height-two maximal ideal** \((p,T)\). A formula for higher Fitting ideals merely **up to height-two equivalence \(\sim\)** does not recover this finite fiber defect.

This is an abstract exact counterexample to a proposed inference, not a constructed Selmer group of any elliptic curve and not a counterexample to the stronger arithmetic theorems with all actual Euler-system hypotheses.

## 3. What the missing integral derived-height/Bockstein index really is

The toy modules have \(T^2X=0\), are Zp-free of rank four, and \(\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbf Q_p}(X/TX)=2\). More generally:

**Theorem 5 (exact rank-two Bockstein determinant).** Let X be a finite free Zp-module of rank four with a Zp-linear operator \(T\) such that \(T^2=0\) and \(T\) has rank two over Qp. Set \(K=\ker T\) (a saturated Zp-lattice of rank two). Then T induces an **injective** Zp-map of rank-two lattices
\[
\beta_T:X/K\hookrightarrow K,\quad \bar x\mapsto Tx.
\]
Its cokernel is finite. There is a noncanonical Zp-module decomposition
\[
\boxed{
X/TX\simeq \mathbf Z_p^2\oplus\operatorname{coker}\beta_T,
\qquad
\mathrm{Fitt}_{2,\mathbf Z_p}(X/TX)
=(\det\beta_T)\mathbf Z_p.
}
\]
**Proof:** K=ker(T:X→X) is saturated because X/K embeds into the torsion-free module X, hence X/K is free rank two. The map β is injective with image TX ⊂ K. The exact sequence
\(0\to K/TX\to X/TX\to X/K\to0\)
splits over Zp because X/K is free; K/TX=coker β. Its Fitting2 is the zeroth Fitting of the finite β cokernel, generated by det β. QED.

For \(X_{\mathrm{good}}\), the Bockstein \(\beta_T\) is a GL2(Zp)-unit matrix, so its determinant is a unit. For \(X_{\mathrm{bad}}\), it is diagonal with entries \((-p,-p)\), hence \(\det\beta_T=p^2\).

This exact computation isolates the missing *integral derived-height datum*: not the characteristic polynomial/determinant of the entire Λ-presentation, nor the nonzero theta quadratic coefficient, but the **index of the integral Bockstein pairing between saturated rank-two lattices**. The actual elliptic-curve Iwasawa Selmer complex is not assumed to satisfy \(T^2X=0\) or to be Zp-free of rank four; an arithmetic construction requires the appropriate *derived* Bockstein/perfect-complex formulation. Do not identify this toy β with an unconstructed Castella–Hsieh derived-height morphism.
